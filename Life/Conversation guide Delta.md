# FIRST CONTACT

1. What’s the weirdest thing you’ve ever seen a guest do with total confidence?
2. If you had to survive one shift using only one tool from your station, what would you grab?
3. What’s your “shift persona” — the version of you that shows up the second you clock in?
4. What’s a menu item or room feature you’d defend to the death, even if nobody else rates it?
5. If this place had a mascot based on its actual vibe, what would it be?
6. What’s the first thing you notice about a new coworker that tells you if you’ll get along?
7. What’s a completely unnecessary skill from a past job that somehow comes in clutch here?

8. GETTING ACQUAINTED

Purpose: reveals working style, humor, and values without asking anyone to open up yet.

1. What’s a moment on shift that turned into an inside joke you still bring up?
2. Who’s someone here — any role — you secretly study because of how good they are at their job?
3. What’s a “small thing done right” you notice in great service, that most guests never clock?
4. What’s your instant tell that a shift is about to go sideways?
5. If you trained your replacement for one day, what’s the one thing you’d insist they understand — not a task, a mindset?
6. What’s a rule at a past job you broke because you thought it was pointless?
7. What’s the most “you” way you’ve ever handled a chaotic moment on the floor?

8. BUILDING RAPPORT

Purpose: invites opinions, small confessions, and glimpses of what people actually care about beyond the job description.

1. What’s a compliment from a guest or coworker that you still think about?
2. What’s something about this industry you’d never admit to someone outside it, but will admit right now?
3. If you could redesign one part of how this place runs — no budget limits — what would you change first?
4. What’s a moment you were proud of yourself here, even if nobody else noticed?
5. Who on the team makes your job easier just by being present, and why?
6. What’s a “guest from hell” story that, looking back, you can actually laugh at?
7. What’s something you do for a coworker that you never mention to anyone?

8. GROWING TRUST

Purpose: touches on pressure, growth, and how people see themselves under it — appropriate for a team that relies on each other.

1. What’s a moment here where you surprised yourself with how well you handled the pressure?
2. What’s something about this job that’s genuinely changed how you deal with people in your regular life?
3. Is there a version of yourself from your first week that you’d want to reassure? What would you tell them?
4. What’s a mistake you made early on that actually made you better at this?
5. What’s something people misjudge about you fast — maybe from how you seem on the floor — that you wish they saw differently?
6. When do you feel most in your element here, and when do you feel like you’re just getting through it?
7. What’s advice you needed when you started that nobody gave you?

8. NEAR-CONNECTION

Purpose: gets into what makes people feel trusted, backed up, and genuinely part of a team.

1. What does it feel like when you know your team actually has your back mid-shift?
2. What’s a moment a coworker made a hard day feel manageable, just by how they showed up?
3. Is there something you’ve never told a manager or teammate that you wish you had?
4. What’s a part of doing this work that took you the longest to feel confident about?
5. When you picture yourself a few years from now, is this industry part of that picture — and why or why not?
6. What’s something you need from a team to actually do your best work, that you rarely say out loud?
7. What would it mean to you if people here remembered the things that matter to you, without you repeating them?

8. DEEP TRUST

Purpose: reserved for teams with real history — resilience, purpose, and what the work actually costs and gives.

1. What’s something you’re proud of that this job took out of you before it gave anything back?
2. If this was your last shift ever, what would you want the team to know about what working here meant to you?
3. What’s a hard season — on shift or off — that this job or this team helped you get through, that people might not know about?
4. What does it feel like, physically, when you finally stop performing “fine” around people you trust here?
5. Is there a way you show up for guests that you wish someone had shown up for you?
6. What’s something about doing this work — feeding people, housing people, taking care of strangers — that quietly means more to you than you let on?
7. If someone on this team saw the parts of you the job usually doesn’t ask for, what do you think they’d find?
