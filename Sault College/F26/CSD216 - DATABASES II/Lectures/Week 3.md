# Analyszing an existing database

> [!question] Very important to understand an organizations existing databases
> - What types of data are being recorded?
> - How is that data being viewed and used? 
> - Hos is the data managed and mainteined?
> - Are there any deficiencies in the current system?
> - How do things need to evolve to support information needs?

There is always a database of some kind...
- Human knowledge base
	- Some information is just in various peoples's minds!
- Paper-based database
	- Notes, reports, forms, printouts, etc.
	- prone to inconsistencies and errors
- Digital database
	- More structured, but often has design deficiencies
---

Do not adopt an existing database design as your starting point
- If the old database didn't have problems, then you wouldn't be getting asked to make a new one!

---
Use existing database(s) to learn about the needs of the organization
- Existing database can help identify necessary fields and tables
- Analysis of existing database can reveal design deficiencies
Begin forming an understanding of
- Organization's overall workflow
- Each individual's specific workflow

- Create a new design that meets the organization's needs

---
1. Analyze how data is collected
2. Analyze how data is presented
3. Interview users and managers regarding 1&2

---
- Copy typical samples of every different source of data and data entry form that is relevant to the database mission
- Take note of exceptions to the typical source

# Conducting interviews

# Subject identification technique
1. Analyze response to open-ended question for subjects
	- Subjects usually revealed in the nouns of a response
	- Subjects identify a person, place, thing, or event
2. Compile a list of subjects as you work through the design process
3. Use list of subjects as basis for further questions
	- Identify (and ignore) duplicates/synonyms
	- Identify (and set aside) characteristics of subjects (will use these later)
	- Identify (and ignore) items irrelevant to the database mission and objectives

---
# Characteristic Identification Technique
1. Pick a subject from the subject list and discuss it
2. Use Subject Identification technique to identify any new subjects
	- Add them to the list
3. Now, look for characteristics of the subject in question
	- Again, usually nouns
	- Often come after a possessive version of subjects (e.g. client's name)
4. Compile list of characteristics as you work through the process
5. Repeat for each subject in the subject list

---
- Subject List and Characteristic list must be separate documents
- Both lists can help identify tables

# Interview Users

1. Identidy type and usage patterns of data used by users
2. Provide detail on samples collected  in previous step
3. Identify types of information required for daily work
